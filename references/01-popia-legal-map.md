# POPIA legal map for builders

Protection of Personal Information Act 4 of 2013 ("POPIA"). Commenced in phases; the substantive conditions and enforcement provisions have been enforceable since **1 July 2021**. Regulations published 14 December 2018 (GNR 1383), **amended 17 April 2025** with immediate effect.

Use this file to cite sections accurately. If a claim isn't here and you're not sure, say so and recommend checking the Act text.

## 1. Key definitions (s1)

| Term | Meaning for builders |
|---|---|
| **Personal information** | Any information relating to an identifiable, living natural person **and, where applicable, an identifiable existing juristic person**. Includes race, gender, sex, pregnancy, marital status, nationality, ethnic origin, colour, sexual orientation, age, health, disability, religion, conscience, belief, culture, language, birth; education, medical, financial, criminal or employment history; **any identifying number, symbol, email address, physical address, telephone number, location information, online identifier**; biometrics; personal opinions; private correspondence; views of others about the person; the name if it appears with other PI or reveals PI. |
| **Data subject** | The person (natural or juristic) the information relates to. |
| **Responsible party** | Determines purpose and means of processing. Usually your client or your company. (GDPR: controller.) |
| **Operator** | Processes PI for a responsible party under contract, without coming under its direct authority. Your hosting, SaaS, email, AI APIs, agencies. (GDPR: processor.) |
| **Processing** | Any operation: collection, receipt, recording, organisation, collation, storage, updating, retrieval, alteration, consultation, use, dissemination, distribution, merging, linking, restriction, degradation, erasure or destruction. |
| **Consent** | Any **voluntary, specific and informed** expression of will. |
| **Unique identifier** | Any identifier assigned to a data subject to identify them for that responsible party's purposes (e.g. SA ID number, account number, customer ID, device ID used for identification). |
| **De-identify** | Delete info that identifies the person, or can be used/manipulated by a reasonably foreseeable method to identify them, or can be linked by such a method to other info that identifies them. **Re-identifiable data is still PI.** |
| **Electronic communication** | Text, voice, sound or image message sent over an electronic communications network, stored until collected. The Regulator's Guidance Note on Direct Marketing (Dec 2024) treats calls (incl. VoIP), push notifications, direct messages as in scope. |
| **Biometrics** | Technique of personal identification based on physical, physiological or behavioural characterisation, including blood typing, fingerprinting, DNA analysis, retinal scanning and voice recognition. |
| **Record** | Any recorded information regardless of form or medium. |

**Juristic persons are data subjects.** A company's registration details, B2B contact lists, supplier records: all in scope. GDPR packs don't cover this.

## 2. Scope and exclusions (s3, s6, s7)

- Applies to processing of PI entered in a record by a responsible party **domiciled in SA**, or not domiciled in SA but **using automated or non-automated means in SA** (unless only forwarding through SA).
- Excluded (s6): purely personal/household activity; de-identified info that can't be re-identified; certain state security / law enforcement; Cabinet, judicial functions. Journalism, literary, artistic expression has a balancing exclusion (s7).
- Exemptions can be granted by the Regulator (s37, s38).

## 3. The 8 conditions

### Condition 1: Accountability (s8)
The responsible party must ensure all conditions are complied with **at the time of determining purpose and means and during processing itself**. Practical meaning: you need a compliance framework, an Information Officer, documented decisions, and evidence.

### Condition 2: Processing limitation (s9 to s12)
- **s9 Lawfulness:** lawfully and in a reasonable manner that doesn't infringe privacy.
- **s10 Minimality:** adequate, relevant and **not excessive** given the purpose.
- **s11(1) Lawful grounds** (need at least one):
  - (a) data subject (or competent person for a child) **consents**;
  - (b) necessary to carry out actions for **conclusion or performance of a contract** with the data subject;
  - (c) complies with an **obligation imposed by law**;
  - (d) protects a **legitimate interest of the data subject**;
  - (e) necessary for a **public law duty** by a public body;
  - (f) necessary for pursuing the **legitimate interests of the responsible party** or a third party to whom info is supplied.
- **s11(2):** burden of proof of consent is on the responsible party. Consent can be **withdrawn at any time** (doesn't affect lawfulness before withdrawal).
- **s11(3):** data subject may **object** at any time on reasonable grounds to processing under (d), (e), (f), and **at any time to direct marketing** (other than s69 unsolicited electronic). After objection: stop processing (s11(4)). Objection uses **Form 1** (Reg 2, amended 2025: free of charge, multiple submission channels).
- **s12 Collection directly from the data subject**, unless exceptions apply (public record, consent to other source, legitimate interest not prejudiced, law enforcement, compliance with law, contract, impractical).

### Condition 3: Purpose specification (s13 to s14)
- **s13:** collect for a **specific, explicitly defined and lawful purpose** related to a function/activity. Make the data subject aware (s18).
- **s14 Retention:** keep no longer than necessary for the purpose, unless: required/authorised by law; reasonably required for lawful purposes related to functions; required by contract; data subject consented. Retention for historical, statistical or research purposes allowed with safeguards.
- **s14(4):** destroy, delete or **de-identify** as soon as reasonably practicable after authorisation to retain ends.
- **s14(5):** destruction/deletion must be done **in a manner that prevents reconstruction in an intelligible form**.
- **s14(6):** restrict processing (not delete) in some cases, e.g. accuracy contested, data subject requests restriction instead of deletion, needed for proof.

### Condition 4: Further processing limitation (s15)
Further processing must be **compatible** with original purpose. Assess: relationship between purposes, nature of info, consequences for data subject, manner of collection, contractual rights. Compatible if data subject consented, info from public record, needed by law, etc. **Repurposing app data to train AI models or for marketing is a classic s15 risk.**

### Condition 5: Information quality (s16)
Reasonably practicable steps to ensure PI is complete, accurate, not misleading and updated where necessary.

### Condition 6: Openness (s17 to s18)
- **s17:** maintain documentation of all processing operations (PAIA s14/s51 manual). In practice: a record of processing activities (ROPA).
- **s18 Notification at collection.** Before collection (or as soon as practicable after, if not collected from the person), take reasonably practicable steps to make the data subject aware of:
  1. the information being collected and, if not from the data subject, the source;
  2. name and address of the responsible party;
  3. purpose of collection;
  4. whether supply is voluntary or mandatory;
  5. consequences of failure to provide;
  6. any law authorising or requiring collection;
  7. if applicable, intention to transfer to a third country and the level of protection there;
  8. any further information necessary for fairness, e.g. recipients, nature/category of info, existence of the right of access and rectification, **right to object** (s11(3)), **right to lodge a complaint with the Information Regulator** and its contact details.

### Condition 7: Security safeguards (s19 to s22)
- **s19(1):** secure integrity and confidentiality of PI by taking **appropriate, reasonable technical and organisational measures** to prevent loss, damage, unauthorised destruction, and unlawful access or processing.
- **s19(2):** take reasonable measures to (a) **identify all reasonably foreseeable internal and external risks**; (b) **establish and maintain appropriate safeguards**; (c) **regularly verify** that safeguards are effectively implemented; (d) **ensure safeguards are continually updated** in response to new risks or deficiencies.
- **s19(3):** have due regard to **generally accepted information security practices and procedures** that apply generally or by industry/professional rules. (This is why mapping to ISO 27001, OWASP ASVS, CIS Controls matters.)
- **s20:** operator processes only with knowledge or authorisation of responsible party; treats PI as confidential.
- **s21:** **written contract** between responsible party and operator ensuring the operator establishes and maintains s19 measures. Operator must **notify the responsible party immediately** of reasonable grounds to believe of unauthorised access.
- **s22 Security compromise notification:** where reasonable grounds to believe PI has been accessed or acquired by an unauthorised person, notify the **Regulator** and the **data subject** (unless identity can't be established) **as soon as reasonably possible** after discovery, taking into account law enforcement needs and measures to determine scope and restore integrity. Delay only if a public body responsible for prevention/detection/investigation of offences or the Regulator determines notification will impede a criminal investigation.
  - Notice to data subject must be **in writing**: by mail, email, prominent website notice, news media, or as the Regulator directs.
  - Content: description of **possible consequences**; measures taken or intended; **recommendations for the data subject** to mitigate; identity of unauthorised person if known.
  - The Regulator may direct publicity. Since **1 April 2025**, notifications to the Regulator go through the **eServices portal** (see 07-breach-response.md).

### Condition 8: Data subject participation (s23 to s25)
- **s23 Access:** a data subject, having provided adequate proof of identity, may request confirmation **free of charge** whether the responsible party holds PI about them; and request the record or description, including identity of third parties who had access. Respond within reasonable time, at a prescribed fee if any, in a reasonable manner and generally understandable form. Fee (if any) and process follow PAIA.
- **s24 Correction/deletion:** request to correct or delete PI that's inaccurate, irrelevant, excessive, out of date, incomplete, misleading or obtained unlawfully; or destroy/delete a record the responsible party is no longer authorised to retain. **Form 2** (Reg 3, amended 2025: free of charge). Must inform data subject of action taken.
- **s25:** access requests per PAIA s18 and s53 procedure.

## 4. Special personal information (s26 to s33)

**Prohibited** to process PI concerning: **religious or philosophical beliefs, race or ethnic origin, trade union membership, political persuasion, health or sex life, biometric information**; or **criminal behaviour** relating to alleged offences or proceedings.

**s27 General authorisations** (one needed):
- (a) **consent** of the data subject;
- (b) necessary for establishment, exercise or defence of a right or obligation in law;
- (c) necessary to comply with an obligation of international public law;
- (d) historical, statistical or research purposes (public interest + appropriate guarantees, or impossible/disproportionate to ask consent);
- (e) info **deliberately made public** by the data subject;
- (f) one of the specific authorisations in s28 to s33.

Specific authorisations (summary): s28 religious/philosophical (religious bodies, institutions, for members); s29 race/ethnic origin (to identify data subjects where essential, or comply with laws/measures protecting or advancing disadvantaged persons, e.g. B-BBEE, Employment Equity); s30 trade union; s31 political persuasion; s32 health or sex life (medical professionals, healthcare institutions, insurers and medical schemes, schools, employers for certain purposes, etc., under confidentiality); s33 criminal behaviour or biometric information (bodies charged by law with applying criminal law, or obtained in accordance with law; employers in accordance with labour law).

**Builder translation:** a race dropdown for B-BBEE reporting can fall under s29; a race field "for marketing segmentation" cannot. Selfie liveness checks and facial matching are biometrics. A gym app storing injuries is health data.

## 5. Children (s34 to s35)

"Child" = natural person **under 18** not legally competent to take decisions without assistance of a competent person.
- **s34:** prohibited to process PI of children unless s35 applies.
- **s35(1)** grounds: (a) **prior consent of a competent person** (parent/guardian); (b) necessary for establishment/exercise/defence of right in law; (c) international public law; (d) historical/statistical/research; (e) deliberately made public by the child with consent of a competent person.
- s35(2): the Regulator may authorise processing in the public interest with conditions (incl. that the responsible party provides parents a means to review and refuse further processing, and doesn't encourage children to divulge more than necessary).

**Builder translation:** if you ask for DOB, school, grade, or target youth (gaming, education, social), you need age gating, verifiable parental consent flows, and minimal data. Prior authorisation (s57) applies to transferring children's info cross-border to countries without adequate protection.

## 6. Information Officer (s55, s56, Reg 4)

- Every responsible party has an **Information Officer** (IO): for private bodies, by default the head (CEO/MD/sole proprietor/partner) under PAIA; may be delegated/authorised. Deputy Information Officers (DIOs) can be designated (s56).
- **s55(2):** IO must **register with the Regulator** before taking up duties. Registration is on the **eServices portal**. Each subsidiary in a group registers separately. The Regulator actively checks this: CJC (May 2026) enforcement notice included non-registration.
- **Reg 4 duties:** ensure a **compliance framework** is developed, implemented, monitored and maintained; ensure a **personal information impact assessment** is done; ensure a **PAIA manual** is developed, monitored, maintained and made available; ensure internal measures are developed with adequate systems to process requests; ensure **internal awareness sessions** are conducted.

## 7. Prior authorisation (s57 to s59)

Obtain authorisation from the Regulator **before** processing if you plan to:
- (a) process **unique identifiers** for a purpose other than the one specifically intended at collection **and** with the aim of **linking** with info processed by other responsible parties;
- (b) process info on **criminal behaviour or unlawful/objectionable conduct** on behalf of third parties;
- (c) process info for **credit reporting**;
- (d) **transfer special PI or children's PI to a third party in a foreign country** that doesn't provide an adequate level of protection.

Notify the Regulator; you may not process until the Regulator completes its investigation (4 weeks, extendable up to 13 weeks) or notifies you a more detailed investigation isn't needed. The Regulator now offers an **online prior authorisation application** (launched 2026). Failure is an offence.

## 8. Direct marketing, directories, automated decisions

- **s69 Direct marketing by unsolicited electronic communication** is **prohibited** unless the data subject:
  - (a) has **given consent**, or
  - (b) is a **customer** of the responsible party, and contact details were obtained **in the context of the sale** of a product or service, for direct marketing of the responsible party's **own similar** products or services, and the data subject was given a reasonable opportunity to object, free of charge and simply, **at collection and on each communication** (if they didn't initially refuse).
  - **s69(2):** you may approach a non-customer for consent **only once**, and not if they previously withheld consent. The request uses the prescribed manner and form: **Form 4** (Reg 6) or substantially similar.
  - **s69(4):** every communication must contain the **identity of the sender** (or person on whose behalf it's sent) and an **address or other contact details to which the recipient may send a request to stop**.
  - Amended Regs (2025): **opt-out mechanisms do not constitute valid consent.**
- **s70 Directories:** subscriber directories need notification and opportunity to object.
- **s71 Automated decision making:** data subject may not be subject to a decision with **legal consequences or that affects them to a substantial degree**, based **solely** on automated processing intended to provide a **profile** (performance at work, creditworthiness, reliability, location, health, personal preferences, conduct). Exceptions (s71(2)): in connection with a contract where the request is met or appropriate measures protect legitimate interests (opportunity to make representations, **sufficient information about the underlying logic**); or governed by law/code of conduct with appropriate measures.

## 9. Cross-border transfers (s72)

A responsible party may not transfer PI to a third party in a foreign country unless:
- (a) the recipient is subject to a **law, binding corporate rules or binding agreement** providing an **adequate level of protection** that effectively upholds principles substantially similar to POPIA's conditions, including onward transfer rules;
- (b) the data subject **consents**;
- (c) transfer is necessary for **performance of a contract** between data subject and responsible party, or pre-contractual measures at the data subject's request;
- (d) necessary for conclusion/performance of a contract **in the interest of the data subject** between the responsible party and a third party;
- (e) transfer is for the **benefit of the data subject**, and it's not reasonably practicable to obtain consent, and if it were, the data subject would likely give it.

As at October 2026 the Regulator has **not published an adequacy list**. A transborder guidance note has been signalled but check its status (10-regulatory-watch.md). Default practice: binding agreement (DPA with POPIA-equivalent clauses) + notice under s18(1)(g).

## 10. Enforcement, offences, fines, civil claims

- **Complaints** (s74): any person may complain (Form 5, Reg 7 as amended 2025). The Regulator can also investigate **on its own initiative** (s76(3)) and conduct **assessments** (s89). From 2026 it writes to organisations in selected sectors asking them to demonstrate compliance.
- **Enforcement notice** (s95): directs specific steps within a period. Appeal to High Court within **30 days** (s97).
- **Offences** (s100 to s106): obstruction of Regulator; failure to comply with enforcement notice; breaching confidentiality; unlawful acts re account numbers (s105); unlawful acts by third parties re account numbers (s106); offences by witnesses; failing to notify processing subject to prior authorisation (s59).
- **Penalties** (s107): fine or imprisonment **up to 10 years**, or both, for the more serious offences (incl. s100, s103(1), s104(2), s105(1), s106(1),(3),(4)); **up to 12 months** for others (e.g. s59, s101, s102, s103(2), s104(1)).
- **Administrative fines** (s109): infringement notice, **up to R10 million**. Instalments possible (Reg 13, 2025). In practice to date, fines have followed **non-compliance with an enforcement notice**.
- **Civil remedies** (s99): data subject (or Regulator on their behalf) may sue for damages for breach of POPIA, **whether or not there is intent or negligence**. Defences limited (vis major, consent, fault of plaintiff, compliance not reasonably practicable, Regulator exemption). Damages may include patrimonial and non-patrimonial loss, aggravated damages, interest, costs.
- **s105/106 account numbers:** a responsible party that contravenes s8/s22 for **account numbers** (bank, card, financial account) in a way that threatens the data subject's interests commits an offence. **Treat account numbers as high-risk data class.**

## 12. Related laws builders hit

| Law | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| **PAIA** (Act 2 of 2000) | s51 **PAIA manual** must be available (website). Access requests. Regulator now oversees PAIA. Annual reporting expected. |
| **ECTA** (Act 25 of 2002) | s43 info a website selling goods/services must display; s45 unsolicited commercial communications must offer opt-out and identify sender. POPIA s69 is stricter for consent. |
| **CPA** (Act 68 of 2008) | s11 right to restrict unwanted direct marketing; pre-emptive block register under the CPA amendment regulations (2026). Registration on the block register **does not replace** POPIA consent. |
| **Cybercrimes Act** (Act 19 of 2020) | Creates cyber offences (unlawful access, interception, data interference, cyber fraud/extortion). Most commenced 1 Dec 2021. **s54** reporting duty (electronic communications service providers and financial institutions: report to SAPS within 72 hours where feasible) had **not commenced** as at last check: verify. |
| **FICA** (Act 38 of 2001) | KYC record retention (generally 5 years after relationship ends). Justifies collecting ID numbers for accountable institutions. |
| **Tax Administration Act** | Records generally 5 years from submission of return. |
| **BCEA / LRA / EEA** | Employee records retention (BCEA 3 years); EEA justifies demographic data for reporting. |
| **Companies Act** | Company records generally 7 years. |
| **National Credit Act** | Credit bureau / credit info rules. |
| **NHA / HPCSA rules** | Health record confidentiality. |
| **Sector rules** | FSCA/PA (Joint Standard 2 of 2024 on cybersecurity for financial institutions), SARB directives for payment system participants, medical schemes rules. |

Retention periods above are commonly cited norms. Confirm the exact period for the client's sector with their attorney or auditor before hard-coding.
