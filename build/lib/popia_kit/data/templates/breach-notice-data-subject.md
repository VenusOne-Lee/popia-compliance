# Security compromise notice to data subjects: {{display_name}}

Use these notices under s22 when there are reasonable grounds to believe personal information was accessed or acquired by an unauthorised person. Notify the Information Regulator through the eServices portal as well (see templates/breach-regulator-checklist.md). Send notices as soon as reasonably possible after discovery.

s22(4) requires the notice to be in writing, by mail, email, a prominent notice on the website, news media, or as the Regulator directs. s22(5) requires enough information for the data subject to take protective measures, including:

1. a description of the possible consequences;
2. the measures taken or intended to address the compromise;
3. recommendations for what the data subject can do to mitigate the effects;
4. the identity of the unauthorised person, if known.

Fill every [bracketed] item before sending. Write plainly. Do not minimise or speculate.

## Version 1: Email or letter

**Subject:** Important: a security incident affecting your personal information

Dear [name],

We are writing to tell you about a security incident at {{company_legal_name}} that affects your personal information.

**What happened**
On [date of discovery] we discovered that [plain description of what happened, for example: an unauthorised person accessed one of our customer databases]. We believe this happened on or around [date or period of the compromise].

**What information was involved**
The information involved is: [list categories, for example: your name, email address, phone number, SA ID number]. [Say clearly what was not involved, if known, for example: Your password and bank details were not affected.]

**Who accessed it**
[If known: The unauthorised person has been identified as [identity]. If not known: We do not yet know who accessed the information.]

**What this could mean for you**
[Possible consequences, for example: Someone could use this information to send you phishing emails or SMS messages that look like they come from us, or to try to impersonate you.]

**What we have done**
- [Measure taken, for example: We closed the access route on [date].]
- [Measure taken, for example: We reset all affected passwords.]
- [Measure intended, for example: We are reviewing our systems with an independent security specialist.]
- We have reported the incident to the Information Regulator. [We have also reported it to the South African Police Service, case number [number], if applicable.]

**What you can do**
- Be careful of emails, SMS or calls asking for personal information, passwords or payments, even if they appear to come from us. We will never ask for your password.
- [If passwords were involved: Change your password for our service, and for any other service where you used the same password. Turn on two-factor authentication.]
- [If SA ID numbers were involved: Consider applying for protective registration with the Southern African Fraud Prevention Service (SAFPS), and consider asking the credit bureaus to place an alert on your file.]
- [If bank or card details were involved: Contact your bank, monitor your statements, and report any transaction you do not recognise.]

**Contact us**
If you have questions, contact our Information Officer, {{io_name}}, at {{io_email}} or {{io_phone}}. [Add a dedicated help line or page, if set up.]

You also have the right to lodge a complaint with the Information Regulator. {{regulator_contact}} Verify the current contact details on https://inforegulator.org.za.

We are sorry this happened. We will update you if we learn anything that changes the advice above.

{{company_legal_name}}
[Date]

## Version 2: SMS (short)

Keep to one or two messages. Point to the full notice. Do not include links that look like phishing: use your known domain only.

> {{company_display}}: A security incident may have exposed your [name, phone, ID number]. We have [contained it]. Watch for scam calls or SMS. [If ID: consider SAFPS protective registration.] Full notice: [{{website}}/security-notice] or {{io_email}}

## Version 3: Website notice

Place prominently on the home page or a linked banner. Keep it up for a reasonable period.

> **Security incident notice**
>
> On [date] {{company_legal_name}} discovered a security incident that affected the personal information of [number or description of affected people].
>
> **Information involved:** [categories].
> **Possible consequences:** [description].
> **Who accessed it:** [identity, if known, or: not yet known].
> **What we have done:** [measures taken and intended]. We have notified the Information Regulator.
> **What you can do:** [recommendations, including SAFPS protective registration if ID numbers were involved].
>
> If you think you are affected, contact {{io_email}} or {{io_phone}}. Last updated: [date].
