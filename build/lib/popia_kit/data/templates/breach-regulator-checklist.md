# Information Regulator notification: what to have ready

Since 1 April 2025, security compromise notifications to the Information Regulator must be made through the **eServices portal**. Email and the old SCN1 form are not accepted. The Information Officer (or a registered Deputy Information Officer) logs the notification.

This checklist lists the information to **have ready** before logging on. It does not reproduce the portal's field names, which may change. Check the portal and the Regulator's current guidance on https://inforegulator.org.za.

Notify as soon as reasonably possible after discovery (s22(1)). Report what you know. Update the notification as facts emerge.

## Before you start

- [ ] Information Officer or Deputy IO is registered and can log on to the eServices portal (BR-02)
- [ ] Incident lead assigned and incident log open
- [ ] Containment under way (keys rotated, access closed, evidence preserved)

## Have ready

### Responsible party
- [ ] Legal name: {{company_legal_name}}
- [ ] Registration number: {{company_reg_number}}
- [ ] Physical address: {{physical_address}}
- [ ] Information Officer name and contact details: {{io_name}}, {{io_email}}, {{io_phone}}
- [ ] Deputy Information Officer details, if any
- [ ] Sector and any sector regulator

### The compromise
- [ ] Date and time (SAST) the compromise happened, or best estimate
- [ ] Date and time it was discovered, and how
- [ ] Plain description of what happened
- [ ] Cause, if known (for example: weak access control, human error, weak password, malware or ransomware, phishing)
- [ ] Whether it is contained, and when
- [ ] Identity of the unauthorised person, if known

### Information and people affected
- [ ] Categories of personal information involved
- [ ] Whether special PI, children's PI, SA ID numbers, account numbers or credentials are involved
- [ ] Number of data subjects affected (exact or estimate, and how estimated)
- [ ] Types of data subjects (customers, employees, children, juristic persons)
- [ ] Whether the information was encrypted, and whether keys were also exposed

### Consequences and response
- [ ] Likely consequences for data subjects
- [ ] Measures taken
- [ ] Measures intended, with dates
- [ ] Recommendations given to data subjects
- [ ] Whether data subjects have been notified, how and when (attach a copy of the notice)
- [ ] If not yet notified: why, and when they will be

### Third parties
- [ ] Whether an operator was involved, its name, and when it notified you (s21(2))
- [ ] Whether SAPS was informed (case number), and whether a public body or the Regulator has determined that notification would impede a criminal investigation (s22(3))
- [ ] Other parties informed: insurer, bank or payment provider, sector regulator, clients

### Supporting documents
- [ ] Incident timeline
- [ ] Redacted evidence summary (no raw PI)
- [ ] Copies of data subject notices (email, SMS, website)

## After logging

- [ ] Save the reference number and confirmation in the incident file
- [ ] Diary a date to submit updates
- [ ] Submit final findings and root cause when known
- [ ] Keep the incident file. The Regulator may assess you later.
